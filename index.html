The Structure

One teaching day. Six practice days.

Every week follows the same rhythm — so tutors and children always know what to expect. Consistency builds confidence.

1
Teach
New concept introduced with story, demo, discussion
2
Guided
Structured practice with support
3
Unplugged
Physical or offline activity — no screen required
4
Explore
Digital exploration — tools, games, AI demos
5
Create
Child builds something original — their own work
6
Share
Explain, reflect, connect — with family or class
7
Assess
Self-check, traffic light, one honest question
🌱 Every week includes an ethics question. Not a separate unit — a habit. Every concept from machines to AI models is connected to the human who built it and the human it affects. That connection is the point.

Placement Assessments

Find the right starting point

Not a test. Not a grade. A conversation with questions that helps any child find their perfect entry point.

🧭

How assessments work

Each assessment is 8–10 questions. Concept-based, not curriculum-intensive. Multiple choice, picture-based, short scenarios, and confidence self-ratings. The output is always a placement recommendation — never a score. Any child can take any assessment at any time, independently, without a tutor present. Nano Tots is open entry — no assessment needed, no barrier, no pressure.

🔬

Nano Tots — Open Entry

No assessment. Every child is welcome here. This is where the journey begins — curiosity is the only requirement.

🧩 Pixel Blockers Entry

Tests: Nano Tots concepts
  • What comes next in this pattern?
  • Why does the order of steps matter?
  • What's the difference between a computer and a human?
  • If you gave a robot the wrong instructions, what would happen?
  • Have you ever used ScratchJr or told a device what to do?
Output: A recommendation "Start at the beginning" · "Start at Week X" · "You're ready for Minecrafters"

⛏️ Minecrafters Entry

Tests: Pixel Blockers concepts
  • In a game, what is a score and why does it change?
  • If a character touches a wall, it should bounce — what kind of thinking is this?
  • What makes a game fair or unfair?
  • What is a loop? Give a real-life example.
  • What is one way AI is different from a normal computer program?
Output: A recommendation "Start at the beginning" · "Start at Week X" · "You're ready for Unicorns"

🦄 Unicorns Entry

Tests: Minecrafters concepts
  • What is training data and why does it matter?
  • If AI only learned from cats, what happens when it sees a dog?
  • Have you ever trained an AI model? What did you learn?
  • What does it mean to design something for a real person?
  • If AI makes a mistake, whose responsibility is it?
Output: A recommendation "Start at the beginning" · "Start at Week X" · "You're a Unicorn — begin here"

Our Principles

What we believe about children and AI

🎨

Creators, not consumers

Every lesson involves making something. Children are always builders — never passive recipients of AI-generated content.

🔒

Safety first, always

Cybersecurity, data rights, online safety, and bias awareness are woven into every level — not taught once and forgotten.

😄

Joy is non-negotiable

If it isn't fun, it isn't working. Every unit is assessed for joy — heavy weeks are balanced with celebration and play.

🧭

Ethics in every lesson

Not a standalone unit. Every single week includes an ethics question — connecting technology to the humans who build and use it.

🌍

AI is human

Every algorithm, every tool, every model was built by a real person who made real choices. Children always learn who built it and why.

🚀

Not a career pipeline

We are not training future employees. We are raising aware, curious, confident young people who understand the world they're growing into.

🌱

Readiness, not age

Levels are competency-based. Ideal ages are guides, not gates. Every child enters where they are — and advances when they're ready.

🏳️

Mistakes are the method

Debugging, failed AI models, wrong predictions — these are the curriculum. Being wrong and fixing it is the most important skill we teach.
